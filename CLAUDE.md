## The project

**Occlusion-Aware Estimation of Mango Fruit Load from Sparse Camera Viewpoints.** Estimating how
many mangoes a tree carries from a short walk around it with a camera, including the fruit no
viewpoint ever sees.

Fruit detectors already find the visible mangoes in an image almost perfectly. A tree hides most
of its own crop, so the visible count is not the crop, and published practice multiplies it by a
constant fitted on a few trees. This project treats it as an abundance-estimation problem of the
kind ecologists use for animals that hide: each camera position is a survey occasion, the pattern
of which viewpoints saw which fruit reveals how detectable fruit are, and the reconstructed canopy
says how much volume no camera could see into. That transfer is the central idea.

## Results so far (simulation only; cite from sim_metrics.json, never from memory)

- Fruit **found**: 37% at one viewpoint, 61% at two, 97% at twelve. Never visible from anywhere:
  24% at one viewpoint.
- **Headline:** reconstruction estimator 3.5% mean absolute error at three viewpoints, against 7.9%
  for the fitted multiplier and 24.5% for plain counting. Three viewpoints with it match twelve
  without.
- Best at every viewpoint count it is defined for; ~2.3x better than the multiplier at three views.
- **Calibration:** the simulator reproduces three published quantities from one setting — fruit
  found at one and two viewpoints (37% vs 40.2%, 61% vs 62.3%) and the correction-factor range
  (1.04–2.99 vs 1.05–2.43).
- **Negative result, found by testing a worse detector:** at two viewpoints with a poor detector the
  estimator reaches 21.9% against the multiplier's 12.5%. Stated as a boundary, not tuned away.
- **Open defect:** interval width correction is the wrong *shape*, not imprecise. Tripling the
  calibration set from 20 to 50 trees barely moved the multipliers; coverage still wrong at two and
  four viewpoints. Uncalibrated intervals are what a user should take.
